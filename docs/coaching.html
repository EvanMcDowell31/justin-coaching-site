First 30 on me

One on one coaching for actors

Work that feels true takes care, time, and listening.

If you love this work, you know the feeling you are chasing. A scene that feels believable. Something honest in what you are expressing, so the story can actually reach somebody.

I do not have this all figured out, and I am still learning every time I tape my own auditions. What I have learned is that listening helps more than forcing ever did. People always say acting is not waiting for your turn to talk, it is listening to the person across from you. But how do you learn to do that? Well, I am glad you asked. Listening is not about the timing of the lines. It is about creating the chemistry between the people in the scene, the thing that makes a scene feel real. You learn that by doing the work, with someone patiently in it with you, more than once a week. That is what this coaching tries to be. Ninety minutes at a time, 2 to 3 times a week, all of it spent on you, at a pace that respects your life.

We all fell in love with stories that felt true

The problem

Reciting is not rehearsing

Most actors I talk to are not untalented and they are not lazy. They prepare alone, they memorize alone, and then they wonder why the tape feels like a copy of the practice instead of the real thing.

Here is the test I give every actor I work with. Be honest with yourself about it.

The tape matches the car

If your tape comes out exactly like your speed throughs in the car, there may not have been much real experiencing of the scene in it yet. You built a delivery and then you repeated it, which is so understandable when you are preparing alone. That is closer to recitation, and we can gently work toward something more alive, even when every line is already right.

The delivery was picked early

The way you say a line is a reflection of how something happened to you, or what something did to you and how it affected you. It is not a delivery you pick in advance. When the delivery is picked first, it can be harder for the scene to really happen to you. Together we look for the version that comes from actually experiencing it.

Nobody is really listening

People work on the timing of the lines like listening is a rhythm thing. It is not. Listening is creating the chemistry between the people in the scene. Without that, two actors can say every word on time and the scene still feels dead.

You are doing it all alone

Almost all of your preparation happens by yourself, with a reader who is just feeding you lines, or no reader at all. Who your reader is, and the conviction they bring, changes everything about what you can find in a scene. Most actors have never once prepared with real help. Then they blame themselves for the result.

It is easy to feel rushed

There is a lot of pressure to just get a tape done and move on to the next thing. I feel that pressure too. Getting the job done matters, and so does getting better over time. You want to be good at what you do. You want to tell stories and actually experience how beautiful that can be. Integrity in the work can be trained, gently and steadily, and you do not have to carry that training alone.

The deeper why

What listening actually is

Listening is not about the timing of the lines. It is creating the chemistry between the people in the scene, the thing that makes a scene feel real. And it is not a skill you turn on and off. A lot of listening is allowing yourself to respond or react instinctually, and training yourself to have control over that part of yourself, and to freely express it through the context of the story you are telling. That is where I help train with you to get better.

Being present is releasing yourself from judgment, and opening yourself to receive, acknowledge, and react to the environment around you. Develop and sharpen those parts of yourself without fear, tell stories the way you want to paint them, and stay directable. Your art can be more than your looks.

My coaching style

Where my coaching is different

You can easily tell when something is manufactured. And it can work. It can honestly, truly work, because not everything that is expressed needs to have the level of stress that producing genuine, authentic emotions can bring. If you are able to convey it without that, there is probably a level of being present and emotional vulnerability that you have trained yourself up to.

My focus is not just on scene study and auditions. It is you. If what you want is a perfectly rehearsed audition, that still gets you the job, and you can tell me that in our fit call. But if you want to train yourself to be a very dimensional human being who can convey truth and carry through the story, let us figure out what that is together.

There also has to be care and integrity in the work. Without it, a scene can look polished and still not quite land. I try to coach toward the other side of that. Work that is believable. Work that carries truth in what you are expressing and emoting. Work you can watch back and feel at peace with, knowing you brought something to life as honestly as you could that day.

My focus is not just on making sure you get bookings.

My focus is to make sure you are getting better.

The why behind the frequency

We start at 2 times a week on purpose

This is the question I get asked the most, so here is the honest answer, in full. Once a week was never going to be enough to change how you work. Twice a week is where the work starts compounding, and 3 times a week is where actors who are chasing a season hard can push it further.

Why I do not do once a week

People ask why I do not do once a week for an hour, like everyone else. Because an hour has never been enough for me to fully take your work apart and put in the volume of training I want to train you through. It is like the gym. Eventually you have to increase the volume, how much you eat, and how much you rest. The more volume you do, the better you get, and the things that normally take people much more time stop being a problem for you. It is always better to be overprepared than underprepared.

What the second session does

One session a week means we spend half of it remembering where we left off. Two sessions a week means the work is still alive in you when we come back to it. We can take something apart in one session and put it back together under real pressure in the next one, while it still matters.

It also means an audition that lands on a Tuesday is not something you face alone on a Wednesday. There is almost always a session close enough to work it properly, with a real reader, and with time to tape it right.

And when you are ready, we build to 3 times a week. That is the intensive pace, for pilot season, for a run of big auditions, or for an actor who knows this is the season to push.

Online is a strength here, not a compromise

I appreciate the self tape as much as I appreciate being in the room. Being in the room is like theatre. You have one shot to give it everything for the audience in front of you. But with everything online, I can show you how to do everything for the self tape, and I can train you specifically for the full 90 minutes. It is not 15 to 20 minutes of your scene and the peanut gallery for everyone else for two hours. This is for you.

We build on what you already have

I focus on what you are doing now, and how we can develop what you are already working with. Meisner, the Method, Uta Hagen, Chubbuck, whoever you trained with, it is all great. We are not throwing any of it away. We are transferring the same long term skills so they take effect in a short period of time, and we stay flexible. This is as much an emotional and mental training ground as it is a physical one. We train everything, and we build your own specific approach.

Inside a session

What ninety minutes actually holds

An hour runs out right when the work gets good. Ninety minutes lets us do the whole job, every time, without rushing the part that matters most.

01

We break the scene down in plain words

Who is this person to you. What is your dynamic with them. What is pushing you to say this, and what prompted you to say it right now. Plain language, no class speak, so the scene becomes something you understand in your body and not just in your head.

02

We experience it, we do not recite it

We work the scene on its feet until it is happening to you, not being repeated by you. Your reader matters here. I read with you myself, with conviction, so the chemistry in the scene has something real to push against. A big part of that work is training control over your instinctual response, and learning to express it freely through the story. That is where believable work comes from, not from performing the idea of a scene, but from living in it truthfully enough that the other person has to respond to you.

03

We tape it and I analyse your tapes in depth

I provide in depth analysis on clients tapes. We watch back together, honestly, the way the other side will watch. What landed, what went quiet, what to send and what to fix while there is still time. Nothing goes out on hope.

04

We work the business side too

Learning the business and staying sharp with it is just as important as the craft work. I want to share what I learned out there and save you from the mistakes I made. You have more control over your career than anyone has told you. We talk about having a real conversation with your agent about the roles you want to go in for, a game plan and expectations, asking for submission reports, understanding who is casting what, why you are going in for a co star instead of the guest star, and how your agent presents you. We also cover the industry itself, like why SAG AFTRA struck and the rules around AI, because an actor who understands the business makes better choices in it.

Where this comes from

I started late, with nothing handed to me

I started acting in my 20s. I was working in sales, but I had always wanted to be an actor.
I finally saw the signs that led me to pursue it, and I allowed myself to learn the business along the way. I had no training and no one in this business. I say that as a good thing now.

I was never groomed by this industry to accept everything as it is, so I learned to question the parts everyone else just accepts. A lot of what I coach came out of that.

  1. My first time on a set was background work in 2016, while I was working at a supplement store. I did not know what hitting your mark meant. I went anyway. That was during a hard reset in my life, and I was looking for solid ground.

  2. Most of my early career was built on free work, copy, credit, meal, and sometimes gas money. Training, auditioning, short films, student films. I did the starving artist path for about seven years in total. I do not recommend it, and part of coaching with me is learning the business well enough that you do not have to live it that way.

  3. Five years after that first background day, I booked a lead role in a feature. It came off a self tape. The thing I had been figuring out alone in my living room turned out to be the thing that changed my career.

  4. I left a smaller agency I had been with about four years, the healthiest dynamic I have ever had with reps, to chase a bigger name. About a year after I signed, that agency went under. It is my biggest career regret, and it taught me the lesson I now teach every actor I coach. Status is optics. Substance is what counts, and you always have a say in how you and your team work together.

  5. I still audition on tape today. I know what it costs to send one, because I still send them. I am not an impatient coach trying to get a tape in just to get it in. I genuinely care about the audition in front of you, and I will give you long term solutions that take effect quickly, not quick fixes that fall apart under pressure.

My guarantee

No minimum

This is my guarantee to you. There is no contract holding you here. You stay because the work is helping you, and you can stop whenever it stops serving you. That is the deal, plain and simple.

What I will never guarantee is bookings, and I want to be honest with you about that. Casting does not work that way, and I do not control who they call back or who they hire. No coach does. What I can stand behind, humbly, is the work itself, and doing my best to help you get better, steadier, and more truthful in it over time.
