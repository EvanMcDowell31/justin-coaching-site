Where this comes from

I started "late", with nothing handed to me

I started acting in my 20s. I was working in sales, but I had always wanted to be an actor, but because of cultural stigmas, I wasn't really encouraged to pursue it.
I finally saw the signs that led me to pursue it, and I allowed myself to learn the business along the way. I had no training and no one in this business. I say that as a good thing now.

I was never groomed by the industry, and I never stayed silent when shady behavior was allowed. So I learned to question the parts everyone else just accepts. A lot of what I coach came out of that.

  1. 2016

    Background work and a supplement store

    My first time on a set was background work in 2016, while I was working at a supplement store. I did not know what hitting your mark meant. I went anyway. That was during a hard reset in my life, and I was looking for solid ground.

  2. The five years nobody posts about

    Free, copy, credit and meal

    Most of my early career was built on free work, copy, credit, meal, and sometimes gas money. Training, auditioning, short films, student films. I did the starving artist path for about seven years in total. I do not recommend it, and part of coaching with me is learning the business well enough that you do not have to live it that way.

  3. 2021

    A lead role, booked off a self tape

    Five years after that first background day, I booked a lead role in a feature. It came off a self tape. The thing I had been figuring out alone in my living room turned out to be the thing that changed my career.

  4. The regret I teach from

    Know when you already have something great

    I left a smaller agency I had been with about four years, the healthiest dynamic I have ever had with reps, to chase a bigger name. About a year after I signed, that agency went under. It is my biggest career regret, and it taught me the lesson I now teach every actor I coach. Status is optics. Substance is what counts, and you always have a say in how you and your team work together.

  5. Right now

    Still taping, still in it

    I still audition on tape today. I know what it costs to send one, because I still send them. I am not an impatient coach trying to get a tape in just to get it in. I genuinely care about the audition in front of you, and I will give you long term solutions that take effect quickly, not quick fixes that fall apart under pressure.

That is why I coach. I want to share what I learned out there and save you from the mistakes I made. And I want you to hear this clearly. You have more control over your career than anyone has told you. Learning the business and staying sharp with it is just as important as the craft, and in here we work both, every week.
